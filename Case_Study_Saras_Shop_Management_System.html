Sara: A Complete Management System for a Muslim Women's Fashion Store

An Arabic-language web system I designed and built from scratch for the Sara store. It brings barcode sales, inventory, factory invoices, accounting and staff payroll together in one place.

The Problem

A fashion store selling many product types (dresses, abayas, niqabs, headscarves, hijab accessories) needed to know at any moment: what's in stock, what sold today, how much is owed to each factory, and how much each employee has earned.

The brief was a simple system that runs in the browser with no server and no subscriptions, that the store's staff can use from day one, and with a visual identity that matches the store's logo.

My Role

I built the whole project myself: requirements analysis with the store owner, interface design from the logo's colors, writing the front end and back end, and testing the sales and accounting logic before handover, followed by ongoing tweaks as the store's workflow evolved.

What I Built

Point of Sale
Search by name or scan a barcode, a cart that holds multiple items in a single sale, and a printed receipt listing every item.
Fashion & Inventory
Product types can be added and removed (dress, abaya, shirt, skirt, niqab, headscarf...), and every item has a code, color, size, wholesale price and profit margin.
Factory Inbound & Outbound
A factory invoice can include existing items, which increase stock, and new items, which are created automatically. Outbound records goods returned to the factory.
Factory Payments
Payments made in installments, with the remaining balance calculated after each one. If you owe 40 and pay 5, 35 remains. A reminder appears before the due date.
Accounting
Daily, monthly and yearly reports, plus a sales and profit summary for each product type.
Staff & Payroll
Each employee's pay = hourly rate × monthly hours + a percentage of their sales, and the commission accrues automatically with every new invoice.

Challenges and How I Solved Them

SpeedA Google Sheets backend is slow by nature

I cached the data in the browser and moved saving to the background: the screen closes immediately and a small message at the bottom confirms the data was added. As a result, moving between sections feels almost instant.

Inventory accuracyA multi-item sale where part of it fails

The backend checks the quantity of every item before deducting anything. If even one item is short, the whole sale is rejected, so inventory is never left half-updated.

Invoice logicA factory invoice mixes existing and new items

Each invoice line either increases the quantity of an existing item or creates a new item with a unique code. All validation happens before anything is written to the sheet, so no invoice is ever saved incomplete.

Data trustDates that shift and text that can break the screen

I stored dates as text so time-zone differences can't shift them, and passed every piece of user input through an escaping function before it's displayed on the page.

Visual Identity

The colors came from the store's own logo: teal as the base, and coral for buttons and alerts. The login page has a light animation, with the logo on the right and the sign-in form on the left.

The Result

  • The store runs on one system instead of scattered tools: sales, inventory, factories, accounting and payroll.
  • Barcode sales save the cashier time, and the receipt prints every item in the sale.
  • The owner knows what's left to pay each factory and when it's due, with no manual calculations.
  • Running cost is close to zero, because everything runs on a Google account.
